Status Exploratory, not peer reviewed

Read this first

These are exploratory results on public research data, from a first run and a fuller second model. They haven't had advisor or peer review, and checks of disease labels against the original papers are still in progress. These numbers may change.

Correction, November 8, 2026: a check against the original papers found that one cancer study, ThomasAM_2019_c, repeated 80 people from another, YachidaS_2019. Earlier versions of this page counted those people twice and included that study as an unseen test. It has been removed and every result here was rerun. With the first run's primary model, the pooled extra-alert rate fell from +2.1 to +0.7 points, and its interval now includes zero. The more accurate models still showed extra alerts. When specificity was matched across models, every model showed extra alerts, including the primary setting. Mean AUROC fell slightly for all models.

This isn't a diagnostic test, a screening tool, or medical advice. Please take health questions to a qualified clinician.

    What these results can't show

    • Why alerts happen: differences could reflect a condition, age, medication, geography, or how a study was run.
    • Confirmed false positives: comparison cohorts usually weren't confirmed to be cancer-free.
    • Clinical performance: these models aren't approved tests and shouldn't guide care.
    • Prediction before diagnosis: samples were collected at or after diagnosis.
    • Independent validation: the second model was designed after first-run results on the same studies were seen, so new cohorts are still needed.
    • Reliable results for small groups: some rows include only a few people, and the Crohn's disease pilot has 43 adults.
    • Truly healthy controls in every study: one Crohn's study's "healthy" metadata label covers non-IBD participants, including some with symptoms.
    • One-study independence: several conditions come from one study, and one large cohort supplies most of the type 2 diabetes data.

    How it was done

    Data came from curatedMetagenomicData, using species-level profiles and one sample per person. Models were tested by leaving out each of 10 cancer studies in turn. One more study was left out because it repeats people from another. The alert threshold aimed for 90% specificity using training data only.

    An analysis plan was recorded before any profiles were downloaded or models were run. Two data errors were found by checks during the run: mixed taxonomic ranks and repeated sample IDs. Those results were discarded, the errors were fixed, and the changes were logged.

    The second model followed a new plan recorded before it was trained. It tried 10 model setups and chose one inside each held-out fold, using only the training studies. It also fitted feature settings inside each training set and added calibration and age-standardized results.

    Earlier studies have tested colorectal cancer models on people with some of these conditions, including type 2 diabetes, Parkinson's disease, and inflammatory bowel disease (Wirbel and colleagues, 2019; Thomas and colleagues, 2019). This project adds more conditions, compares each with healthy controls from the same study, pools the results, and reports cancer stage alongside them.
