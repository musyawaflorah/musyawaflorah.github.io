Working project title

A Disease-Specificity Benchmark for Stool Microbiome AI in Colorectal Cancer

Status Protocol development and first exploratory results

Research question

How often do stool microbiome models built to detect colorectal cancer raise false alerts for people with Crohn’s disease and other conditions, and can adding disease information improve specificity while preserving cancer sensitivity?

Why ask it

A model trained only on colorectal cancer and control samples has not been asked to tell cancer apart from other conditions that affect the gut. Inflammatory bowel disease, including Crohn’s disease, is also associated with changes in the gut microbiome. This project sets out to measure whether that leads to false alerts across conditions, with Crohn’s disease as a pilot, and whether adding disease information helps without causing more cancers to be missed.

Where the project stands

Candidate data resource

The candidate resource is the public processed stool microbiome profiles and associated metadata in curatedMetagenomicData. It was used for the first exploratory run. Dataset suitability and patient-level labels are still being checked against the original papers.

Scope and limitations

This is exploratory research, not a clinical diagnostic service. It is not a test, a screening tool, or medical advice, and I do not accept samples or interpret individual results. Please take any health concerns to a qualified clinician.

The project makes no claim of accuracy or clinical benefit, and it is not intended to replace biopsy or any other clinical procedure.
